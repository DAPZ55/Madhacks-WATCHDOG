# Chronic Problems in Construction Excavation & Earthworks Addressable by Visual AI / Sensors

Research date: 2026-11-14. Many official datasets lag 1-2 years; data year is noted on each claim. About 18 searches/fetches were made. Two primary pages (CGA 2025 DIRT summary on trenchlesstechnology.com, CGA locating special report) returned HTTP 403, so some CGA figures come through trade press.

## Which problems cause the most deaths, injuries, cost, or delay?

### Takeaway
By dollars, underground utility strikes are the largest chronic excavation problem by far. CGA models about $83.2B/yr in total economic impact, about 197k reported damages in 2024, and the trend is getting worse, not better. By deaths, trench cave-ins (about 20/yr average in the US, with spikes to 39 in 2022 and 17 in 2025) and heavy-equipment struck-by/backover/rollover deaths are the leading excavation killers. Unforeseen subsurface conditions are a persistent but smaller and harder-to-measure cost driver (about 1-7% of highway project cost; larger on underground megaprojects).

### Cited Findings

**1. Underground utility strikes (811 / locating / as-builts)**
- CGA's 2024 DIRT report analyzed 196,977 unique damage reports from 2024. The industry is not on track for its "50-in-5" goal (cut damages 50% in 5 years) — [Compact Equipment](https://compactequip.com/business/cga-releases-2024-dirt-report-findings-highlights-need-for-industry-improvements/); [Utility Contractor](https://utilitycontractormagazine.com/cga-dirt-report/)
- CGA's damage index rose from 94.0 (2023) to 96.7 (2024), meaning it is moving the wrong way — [Compact Equipment](https://compactequip.com/business/cga-releases-2024-dirt-report-findings-highlights-need-for-industry-improvements/)
- Root causes are concentrated and stable. The top 10 root causes were 85% of damages in 2024 and 86% in the 2025 data. Utility work (especially water/sewer and telecom/CATV) dominated 9 of the top 10 root causes in 2024 — [Compact Equipment](https://compactequip.com/business/cga-releases-2024-dirt-report-findings-highlights-need-for-industry-improvements/); [Trenchless Technology 2025 summary](https://trenchlesstechnology.com/cga-releases-2025-dirt-data-summary-trends/) (snippet via search; page 403)
- 2025 preliminary data: trade press describes damages as worsening, with telecom networks hit most. The leading root cause is still not contacting 811 before digging. Excavators in CGA's Damage Prevention Institute cut their attributable damage rate by more than 11% from 2023 to 2025 — [Cabling Install](https://www.cablinginstall.com/design-install/news/55396303/underground-utility-damage-worsened-in-2025-and-telecom-networks-got-hit-the-most); [Trenchless Technology](https://trenchlesstechnology.com/cga-releases-2025-dirt-data-summary-trends/)
- Economic impact: CGA's model gives $83.2B/yr total. Only $4.9B (5.9%) is direct repair. The rest: community/environmental $32.6B, business disruption $31.6B, operational response $7.8B, human impacts including injuries and fatalities $6.2B. Moderate-severity incidents account for $64.5B and urban incidents for $77.9B. The national figure adjusts for unreported damages. DIRT reporting is voluntary, so state figures are minimums — [Environment+Energy Leader](https://environmentenergyleader.com/stories/underground-utility-damage-creates-832b-in-economic-impact,140943)
- One trade source estimates a single strike can cost a contractor about $56k (repairs, downtime, fines, claims, legal). This is unverified secondary data — [AZ Big Media](https://azbigmedia.com/real-estate/commercial-real-estate/construction/how-construction-teams-reduce-underground-utility-strike-risks/)
- CGA released a 2024 special report on locating-practice damages, citing risks and opportunities in locating. Full figures were not retrieved (403) — [Underground Infrastructure](https://undergroundinfrastructure.com/news/2024/june/common-ground-alliance-report-identifies-risks-opportunities-for-improving-underground-utility-locating-practices)
- Bad records cost money. The FHWA-commissioned Purdue study (about 1999-2000; 71 projects, more than $1B construction value) found $4.62 saved per $1 spent on Subsurface Utility Engineering, which FHWA calls a minimum. Later studies range from $3.41 (Toronto) to $22.21 (PennDOT) — [FHWA](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/programadmin/purdue.cfm); [WGI whitepaper](https://publications.wginc.com/hubfs/2020_WP_SUE%20Now%20Save%20Money%20Time%20Later.5.pdf)

**2. Trench collapses / cave-ins**
- BLS: 166 workers died in trench cave-ins from 2011 to 2018, about 21/yr — [Equipment World](https://www.equipmentworld.com/workforce/safety/article/15293290/trench-collapses-kill-13-workers-so-far-in-2022); BLS confined-spaces factsheet gives 168, with 135 in private construction — [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/iif/factsheets/fatal-occupational-injuries-confined-spaces-2011-19.htm)
- 2022 was a record: 39 trench/excavation deaths (CPWR), up from 15 in 2021, a 160% increase — [Safety+Health / CPWR](https://safetyandhealthmagazine.com/articles/25402-trenching-and-excavations-deaths-rose-160-in-2022-cpwr); [Equipment World](https://www.equipmentworld.com/regulations/safety-compliance/article/15305147/osha-35-trench-deaths-in-2022-more-than-double-2021s-toll)
- OSHA credits enforcement (the National Emphasis Program) for the drop to 15 deaths in 2023 and 12 in 2024 (partial year, as of the Nov 2024 release) — [OSHA/DOL release](https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/releases/osha/osha20241104-0)
- Revised counts are 13 deaths in 2024 and 17 in 2025, the highest since 2022, so the decline did not hold — [Jordan Barab, Confined Space, Jan 2026](https://jordanbarab.com/confinedspace/2026/01/08/trench-collapse-deaths-ring-out-the-old-year/)
- Construction workers were 85% of fatal trenching injuries from 2011 to 2021 (CPWR) — via [Safety+Health](https://safetyandhealthmagazine.com/articles/25402-trenching-and-excavations-deaths-rose-160-in-2022-cpwr)
- Media counts undercount, since state-plan OSHA deaths are often unreported — [Equipment World](https://www.equipmentworld.com/regulations/safety-compliance/article/15668575/18-workers-die-in-trench-collapses-since-record-deadly-2022)

**3. Heavy equipment struck-by / backover / blind spots**
- On excavation work sites, 253 heavy-equipment-related deaths occurred from 1992 to 2002 (NIOSH/BLS CFOI; old data). Backhoes and trucks were involved in half. Rollovers were the main killer of operators, and struck-by (especially backing vehicles and loads/parts) was the main killer of workers on foot — [NIOSH, CDC Stacks](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/185809)
- In 2003-2005, being struck by vehicle loads or parts (especially excavator buckets) caused 12 of 159 (8%) deaths analyzed — [CPWR Construction Chart Book](https://www.elcosh.org/document/1059/270/d000038/sect39.html)
- In road construction, 143 of 443 struck-by fatalities (2003-2010) involved a backing vehicle or equipment. Among worker-on-foot deaths in work zones, backing incidents were 51% — [OSHA Preventing Backovers](https://www.osha.gov/preventing-backovers) and NIOSH via search summary
- Struck-by caused 804 construction worker deaths from 2011 to 2015, the most of any industry (CPWR) — [Safety+Health](https://safetyandhealthmagazine.com/articles/reducing-backover-incidents-2)
- NIOSH flags a quick-coupler hazard: buckets detach from excavator quick couplers and strike workers (e.g., a 2021 California fatality) — [NIOSH Science Blog 2022](https://blogs.cdc.gov/niosh-science-blog/2022/03/10/stuck-by-injuries)

**4. Unforeseen ground conditions (soil, groundwater, contamination: "differing site conditions")**
- NCHRP (2016): geotechnical change orders averaged 1.3% of estimated construction cost, and direct geotechnical overruns averaged about 3%. Subsurface issues were 7% of claims/change orders/overruns by cost, but much higher on individual projects. Differing site conditions is the most frequent claim heading — [NAP / NCHRP Synthesis](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/21926/chapter/7); [summary](https://energyskeptic.com/2016/cost-overruns-on-roads-from-subsurface-conditions/)
- Large underground infrastructure averages about 40% cost overrun (attributed to Flyvbjerg; secondary source, not verified) — [Roads & Bridges](https://roadsbridges.com/road-construction/news/55314863/proactive-risk-management-tackling-underground-uncertainties-upfront)
- Agencies say subsurface investigation cannot fully eliminate claims risk — [NAP](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/21926/chapter/7)

**5. HDD (horizontal directional drilling): strikes, frac-outs, cross-bores**
- Telecom caused about 60% of directional-drilling damage in Minnesota DIRT data (2020-2022) — [MN House doc](https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/kHOxo95mpkisxVIgl9PsLQ.pdf); CGA has an HDD-specific DIRT section — [CGA 2021 DIRT HDD](https://dirt.commongroundalliance.com/2021-DIRT-Report/Horizontal-Directional-Drilling)
- Cross-bores: in a PHMSA/NAPSR 2014 meta-analysis, 14 of 23 responding states (61%) reported incidents or near-misses from gas cross-bores in sewers, and 6 states reported injuries — [PHMSA](https://www.phmsa.dot.gov/sites/phmsa.dot.gov/files/docs/technical-resources/pipeline/gas-distribution-integrity-management/66016/metaanalysiscrossborepractices07102014-final-r3.pdf)
- About 3-5 cross-bores per km of mainline sewer were found in high-risk areas inspected — [Trenchless Australasia](https://www.trenchless-australasia.com/2017/12/11/locating-the-hazard/)
- Frac-outs (drilling fluid escaping to the surface) are usually minor and near the bore entry/exit, but some have damaged roads, water pipes, and power boxes — [Utility Magazine AU](https://utilitymagazine.com.au/what-is-a-frac-out-in-hdd/)

### Inferences
- Ranking by total impact: (1) utility strikes, on cost and frequency; (2) trench cave-ins, on deaths per incident (often fatal, fully preventable); (3) equipment struck-by/backover, a steady death toll; (4) unforeseen ground conditions, on cost and delay; (5) HDD cross-bores, low frequency but catastrophic (gas explosions, latent risk years later).
- Utility strikes have persisted for decades. One-call/811 has existed since the 1970s, and the national 811 number since 2007. Even so, damages are flat-to-rising, which suggests process and enforcement alone have plateaued. That plateau is the opening for technology.
- Trench deaths swing with enforcement intensity, not technology. Protective systems (shoring, shields, sloping) already exist and work. The failure is compliance at the moment of entry, which is an ideal fit for visual monitoring.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the full CGA 2025 DIRT / State of Damage Prevention report (403). Exact 2025 damage count and root-cause percentages (e.g., "locating practices not sufficient," "facility not marked due to incorrect records," "no notification") are not verified here.
- No current (post-2015) excavator-specific struck-by fatality count was found. The CPWR Data Center / CFOI query tool would have it.
- No national counts were found for frac-outs, cross-bore explosions, or injuries from utility strikes (CGA's $6.2B "human impacts" is dollars only).
- No quantified national data on dewatering/groundwater surprises or contaminated-soil discoveries specifically, beyond the aggregated "differing site conditions" figures.

## What has been tried (GPR, EMI, LiDAR, drones, machine control, camera AI) and where does it fall short?

### Takeaway
Each incumbent sensing method has a hard physics or adoption ceiling. EM locators can't see plastic. GPR fails in clay and wet soil and gives poor depth. Machine control is still a minority of excavators. Camera people-detection exists from OEMs and Blaxtair but lacks independent validation and covers only the machine's own surroundings. Well-funded players (Exodigo, about $214M raised) attack pre-dig mapping with multi-sensor AI. Nobody clearly owns at-the-bucket or at-the-trench real-time verification.

### Cited Findings

**Utility locating: EMI and GPR**
- EM locators detect only conductive lines (no plastic or clay pipe). Contractors rely on tracer wire for plastic — [GX Contractor](https://www.gxcontractor.com/equipment/article/13027950/equipment); [Severn Partnership](https://www.severnpartnership.com/?p=9241)
- University of Pretoria field trials: GPR was "reasonably effective" in clay loam but depth readings were inaccurate and consistently too shallow. GPR was ineffective in clay, and could not detect a 75 mm PVC pipe at about 345 mm depth in one soil — [U. Pretoria Innovate 2020](https://issuu.com/universityofpretoria/docs/innovate_15_2020/s/11079642)
- Clay, water, and minerals raise conductivity and cut GPR penetration. Lower frequency goes deeper but resolves less. Broad beamwidth makes closely spaced pipes hard to separate. In disturbed ground, radar may see trench walls rather than the pipe — [ldolphin GPR limits](https://www.ldolphin.org/GPRLimits.html); [GX Contractor](https://www.gxcontractor.com/equipment/article/13027950/equipment)
- GPR depth accuracy is typically "several feet or less," and achievable depth is unknown until a site is scanned — [ldolphin](https://www.ldolphin.org/GPRLimits.html)

**Multi-sensor AI mapping (commercial)**
- Exodigo closed a $96M Series B in July 2025 ($214M total since 2021, per company). It combines AI, multiple sensors, and 3D imaging to map buried utilities non-invasively. Reported valuation is $600-700M (figures vary by source) — [Informed Infrastructure](https://informedinfrastructure.com/post/exodigo-brings-ai-efficiency-to-infrastructure-industry-closes-96-million-series-b); [T&D World](https://tdworld.com/intelligent-undergrounding/news/55303694/ai-underground-mapping-company-raises-96-million-to-expand-us-and-global-reach)
- Exodigo has received US federal funding for grid-modernization mapping — [Geo Week News](https://www.geoweeknews.com/articles/exodigo-power-grid-federal-funding-underground-utilities/)
- A UK underground mapper raised £82M for UK expansion (likely Exodigo; not confirmed in snippet) — [The Engineer](https://theengineer.co.uk/articles/underground-mappers-raise-82m-for-uk-expansion)
- 4M Analytics (Austin, founded 2019) does AI utility mapping from records and imagery. The last known round was $30M (Sep 2022); no 2025 round was found — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/4m-analytics-vs-exodigo)
- Urbint (damage-prevention risk scoring for 811 tickets) now redirects to Itron, indicating it was acquired — observed redirect from [urbint.com blog](https://www.urbint.com/blog/2024-dirt-report-damage-rates-rise-2.7-heres-the-path-forward)

**Equipment proximity / camera AI**
- Cat Detect People Detection for excavators uses 3 cameras with up to 270° coverage and 9 m (29.5 ft) maximum range, with visual and audible alerts. It is not compatible with Cat Command remote control and supplements, not replaces, procedures — [Cat](https://www.cat.com/en_GB/products/new/technology/detect/detect/117380.html)
- Blaxtair (Arcure) uses AI on a 3D camera to classify humans vs objects (fewer false alarms), configurable to 360° on excavators. It claims "up to 80% fewer near misses," a vendor claim with no independent validation found — [Blaxtair](https://blaxtair.com/en/blog/excavator-blind-spot-safety-systems)
- Academic work: Zhang & Zhang (Baidu Research; arXiv 2110.03083, ISARC 2021) built a camera-only pipeline that detects machines and workers, estimates excavator pose, recognizes activity, and warns of collisions — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03083v3); [ISARC](https://www.iaarc.org/publications/fulltext/007%20ISARC%202021%20Paper%20106.pdf)
- A Nature research summary notes YOLO-based systems are widely studied for PPE and hazardous-zone entry on construction sites — [Nature Research Intelligence](https://www.nature.com/research-intelligence/nri-topic-summaries/computer-vision-and-deep-learning-applications-in-construction-safety-management-micro-186794)
- No independent field validation of the commercial people-detection systems was found.

**Machine control / grade control / productivity**
- Machine-control adoption surveys: 39% of respondents use some type. Another survey (2023 State of the Industry) found 27% use OEM machine control, 5% budgeting, 11% planning. Excavator adoption trails dozers and graders; Topcon estimated about 10% of excavators in Europe. Scandinavia is the highest-adoption region — [Construction Equipment](https://www.constructionequipment.com/grade-control-gains-ground); [Topcon](https://www.topconpositioning.com/articles/machine-control---the-basics); [Construction Briefing](https://www.constructionbriefing.com/news/what-are-the-benefits-of-machine-control-in-construction/8020317.article)
- Vendor-claimed 30-50% time savings from machine control (marketing, not independent) — [International Construction](https://www.international-construction.com/news/what-are-the-benefits-of-machine-control-in-construction/8020317.article)

**Pile installation / autonomy**
- Built Robotics RPD 35 (modified Cat 335) combines survey, pile distribution, driving, and as-built data capture. Company claims more than 300 piles/day with a 2-person crew, and 3-5x faster than manual — [Built Robotics](https://builtrobotics.com/solutions/solar); [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2023/04/27/robotic-pile-driver-faster-than-manual-versions-says-us-startup)
- Blattner is deploying dozens of Built robots on US solar projects (Sep 2025) — [Sumitomo VISIONS](https://www.sumitomocorp.com/en/us/scoa/publications/VISIONS_10_2025_2). At Meta's Hyperion data center, Built machines reportedly handle more than half of pile driving (July 2026) — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/meta-hyperion-data-center-robots/)

**Unforeseen ground conditions**
- NCHRP: minimum subsurface-investigation standards reduce claims, but investigation cannot remove the risk entirely. Geotechnical Baseline Reports and DSC clauses allocate risk contractually, but disputes stay contentious — [NAP](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/21926/chapter/7); [Tunneling Online](https://tunnelingonline.com/contracts-risk-building-blocks-success/)

### Inferences
- **Utility strikes, the gap:** Exodigo and 4M work before the dig (survey/mapping). The 811 locator paints marks on the ground. Then the excavator digs, often days later, with marks faded, scraped, or misread. Nothing in the evidence above clearly provides continuous at-the-bucket verification: comparing the bucket position against painted marks, tolerance zones (typically 18-24 in. each side, varying by state), and ticket data, or alerting when exposed pipe or tape appears in the excavation.
- **Trench cave-ins, the gap:** Commercial camera AI focuses on people around machines, not on trench state. No commercial product was found that verifies "is a protective system present, is the trench deeper than 5 ft, is a worker in it, is spoil at least 2 ft back from the edge, is there a ladder within 25 ft." These are OSHA 1926 Subpart P criteria, which are visually checkable. This is also academically underexplored: the targeted search returned no trench-specific CV paper.
- **Struck-by, the gap:** OEM systems ship on new premium machines. The legacy fleet (mostly older, mixed-brand machines, and rentals) is the retrofit market. Blaxtair occupies it but at premium hardware cost; a cheap camera plus edge AI retrofit is plausible.
- **Volume/cut-fill and progress monitoring:** Drone photogrammetry for stockpile volumes and cut/fill (DroneDeploy, Propeller, Pix4D, Komatsu Smart Construction) is a mature, crowded category based on my background knowledge. It is not verified by sources in this round. Machine-mounted or phone-based continuous tracking between drone flights is a plausible niche.

### Gaps
- Komatsu Smart Construction, Trimble, Pointerra, and ULC Robotics specifics (capabilities, pricing, adoption) were not retrieved in this research round.
- No sourced accuracy figures for drone stockpile and cut/fill measurement were found.
- No evidence was found on HDD bore-tracking (walkover locator) error rates or on commercial frac-out detection systems.
- No sourced evidence on AI soil-type classification from images, or on in-field contamination detection (PID/XRF plus vision). These remain unverified opportunities.
- Pile installation QA beyond Built Robotics (e.g., PDA dynamic testing, vision-based pile verticality or position check) was not researched.

## Where are the clearest openings for a camera- or cheap-sensor-based AI product?

### Takeaway
The strongest small-team openings are vision tasks around things that are already visible but under-monitored. These include trench compliance (protective system, depth, spoil, ladder, worker-in-trench), utility marks and tolerance-zone awareness at the bucket, and retrofit blind-spot people detection for legacy equipment. Subsurface sensing (GPR/EMI fusion) and HDD tracking are physics-limited and capital-heavy, so they are poor fits for a hackathon.

### Cited Findings
- The problem size supports these openings: about $83.2B/yr utility-damage impact, 85-86% of damages from 10 repeated root causes — [E+E Leader](https://environmentenergyleader.com/stories/underground-utility-damage-creates-832b-in-economic-impact,140943); [Compact Equipment](https://compactequip.com/business/cga-releases-2024-dirt-report-findings-highlights-need-for-industry-improvements/)
- Trench deaths rebounded to 17 in 2025 — [Confined Space](https://jordanbarab.com/confinedspace/2026/01/08/trench-collapse-deaths-ring-out-the-old-year/)
- Camera-only excavator pose and activity recognition plus collision warning is demonstrated academically — [arXiv 2110.03083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03083v3)
- Commercial people detection exists (Cat Detect 270°/9 m; Blaxtair 360°) but is unvalidated independently — [Cat](https://www.cat.com/en_GB/products/new/technology/detect/detect/117380.html); [Blaxtair](https://blaxtair.com/en/blog/excavator-blind-spot-safety-systems)
- Exodigo's $214M raise shows investor appetite for AI subsurface mapping, but it is a capital-intensive hardware and sensor play — [Informed Infrastructure](https://informedinfrastructure.com/post/exodigo-brings-ai-efficiency-to-infrastructure-industry-closes-96-million-series-b)
