# Tunneling and Underground Construction: Chronic Problems Addressable by Visual AI / Sensors

Research date: 2026-11-14. Sources are web search and fetch results, mostly secondary or abstracts. Older data is flagged with its year. Feasibility ratings are my own inferences, not sourced facts.

## Which problems cause the biggest overruns, accidents, or delays (with numbers and case studies)?

### Takeaway
Overruns and delays come mostly from unforeseen ground conditions and obstructions ahead of the face. Seattle's Bertha is the canonical case: one unidentified steel well casing, then 867 days of delay. They also come from ground loss and sinkholes in urban soft ground, which keep recurring in 2024-2025 in Bangkok, Bengaluru, Seoul, Hanoi and LA, and from TBM entrapment and cutter-maintenance downtime. Megaproject overruns such as HS2 are driven mostly by management and estimation failures, not tunneling technology. A small team should not claim it can fix those.

### Cited Findings
**Portfolio-level overrun statistics**
- In Flyvbjerg's 258-project transport sample (2003), "fixed links" (bridges and tunnels together) averaged 34% cost escalation (N=33, sd=62). Rail averaged 45%, roads 20%, and all project types 28%. This is not a tunnel-only figure. — [Flyvbjerg et al. 2003, Transport Reviews](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:transr:v:23:y:2003:i:1:p:71-88)
- Flyvbjerg data quoted by KUOW (2018) says mega-tunnel projects average 37% over budget. Seattle SR 99's schedule overrun of more than 50% was called "twice as bad" as the average, which implies an average schedule overrun of about 25%. That is an inference from the article, not a stated figure. — [KUOW, Dec 2018](https://kuow.org/stories/what-grade-does-seattle-s-new-waterfront-tunnel-get)
- A 2026 study of 27 completed Australian tunnel projects found an average cost overrun of 46.6%, with wide variability. It cites the Channel Tunnel at about 80% over and Boston's Big Dig at about 200%. I saw this only as a search summary and did not verify the full text. — [Flyvbjerg/overrun search cluster; tunnelsandtunnelling.com](https://tunnelsandtunnelling.com/?p=4554)

**Seattle SR 99 "Bertha" (2013-2019): an obstruction ahead of the face**
- Bertha stalled after striking a 110-ft steel pipe on Dec 3, 2013. The pipe was a 2002 groundwater test-well casing, and the contractor said it was not on the agency's tunnel plans. — [KIRO7](https://www.kiro7.com/news/local/as-seattle-tunnel-partners-seeks-480-million-for-berthas-breakdown-a-judge-deals-the-contractor-a/464074002); [King5](https://www.king5.com/article/news/local/seattle/state-loses-bid-to-block-contractors-argument-in-bertha-breakdown/281-499822107)
- The contractor (STP) filed a $480M interim claim for repair. A jury rejected about $330M of STP claims and awarded WSDOT $57.2M for 867 days of delay. The WA Supreme Court later declined STP's appeal. — [Equipment World](https://www.equipmentworld.com/better-roads/article/14971884/wsdot-wins-572m-suit-over-berthas-breakdowns-during-alaskan-way-viaduct-project); [Spokesman-Review 2022](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2022/oct/14/wa-supreme-court-hands-wsdot-a-77m-win-in-highway-)
- The WA Supreme Court ruled that builders' risk insurance does not cover delay costs. — [WA Courts PDF](https://www.courts.wa.gov/content/publicupload/eclips/2022%2009%2016%20Insurers%20dont%20have%20to%20pay%20for%20Highway%2099%20tunnel%20delays%20WA%20Supreme%20Court%20rules%20.pdf)
- Bertha's repairs pushed the tunnel about $200M over budget, according to the headline. Fixing the stuck TBM took "a couple years". The project was originally scoped as 6 years from 2009 and opened in Feb 2019. — [Equipment World](https://www.equipmentworld.com/better-roads/article/14965523/berthas-repairs-push-seattles-sr99-tunnel-200-million-over-budget-and-it-could-get-worse); [KUOW](https://kuow.org/stories/what-grade-does-seattle-s-new-waterfront-tunnel-get)

**HS2 (UK) and Crossrail: megaproject overruns**
- The NAO (June 2026) says DfT now expects HS2 to cost £87.7bn-£102.7bn, about double the 2020 estimate. London-Birmingham services may not run until 2039, up to 13 years late. The NAO attributes most of the increase to "cost underestimation, inefficient delivery and scope changes". — [NAO press release](https://www.nao.org.uk/press-releases/dft-makes-progress-but-must-get-reset-of-hs2-right-this-time-following-past-failures/); [NAO report PDF](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/high-speed-two-reset.pdf); [Construction News](https://www.ukconstructionmedia.co.uk/news/hs2-reset-must-succeed-as-costs-double-and-delays-mount-nao-warns/)
- £3.8bn has been spent at Euston, with another £4.1bn anticipated, including completing tunnelling from Old Oak Common. — [NAO via search summary](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/high-speed-two-reset-summary.pdf)
- Crossrail spent up to £200M on instrumentation and monitoring. About 16,000 buildings, tunnels or utilities were potentially affected by tunnelling. — [IET, Crossrail improving performance](https://www.theiet.org/media/9513/crossrail-improving-performance-of-delivering-infrastructure.pdf)

**Ground loss, sinkholes and face instability (recurring, 2024-2025)**
- Bangkok, 24 Sep 2025: a massive sinkhole opened on Samsen Road in front of Vajira Hospital. It was linked to a weak junction between the metro tunnel and a station, and residents had seen water seeping from the road beforehand. Reports put it at roughly 50 m. — [InterRisk Thai](https://www.interriskthai.co.th/blog/massive-sinkhole-on-samsen-road-in-bangkok/); [GeoEngineer](https://geoengineer.org/news/repair-of-massive-50-meter-sinkhole-in-bangkok-enters-new-challenge-after-structural-breach); [Construction Briefing](https://www.constructionbriefing.com/news/video-bangkoks-governor-suggests-metro-construction-could-have-caused-dramatic-street-collapse/8085902.article)
- Bengaluru Pink Line, June 2024: TBM "Tunga" was stopped after a surface sinkhole, the third on the line since 2023. An air pocket or loose soil was suspected. — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fkarnataka%2Fbengaluru%2Fsetback-for-metro-s-pink-line-tbm-tunga-stopped-due-to-sinkhole-3084087)
- Seoul, March 2025: a Myeongil-dong sinkhole killed one person, and an investigation found combined failures. Separately, a Gwangmyeong subway site collapsed on 11 Apr 2025. — [GeoEngineer](https://www.geoengineer.org/news/investigation-reveals-combined-failures-behind-seoul-sinkhole-collapse)
- Hanoi: a mud eruption during TBM work on the Nhon-Hanoi Station metro forced a halt to keep the face stable. — [VietnamNet](https://vietnamnet.vn/en/mud-erupts-from-metro-tunnel-in-hanoi-residents-evacuate-homes-2405533.html)
- Los Angeles, July 2025: a partial wastewater tunnel collapse stranded a TBM, and the project was reported frozen. — [Construction Owners](https://www.constructionowners.com/news/la-tunnel-project-stalled-after-collapse)
- One geo-monitoring blog argues that across these cases the warning signals existed but the response procedure did not. This is opinion. — [geomechanics.io](https://www.geomechanics.io/news/article/sydney-metro-west-tunnelling-complete-geotechnical-risk-shifts-for-engineers)

**Squeezing ground and TBM entrapment**
- Shielded TBMs risk entrapment in squeezing ground, which entails long delays and major risk. In critical stretches, squeezing can call the feasibility of a TBM drive into question. — [Robbins Difficult Ground brochure](https://www.robbinstbm.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/DifficultGround_Brochure.pdf); [ITA WTC paper](https://library.ita-aites.org/wtc/261-evaluating-the-possibility-of-shield-jamming-in-squeezing-grounds.html)
- Kunming, China (IAEG 2006): squeezing, water inflow and TBM blocking forced a realignment of the tunnel after 4 km. Smectite-rich fault gouges contributed. — [IAEG 717](https://media.geolsoc.org.uk/iaeg2006/PAPERS/IAEG_717.PDF)
- Uluabat, Turkey: TBM stoppages themselves worsen shield squeezing. A "squeezing index" was derived from changes in thrust force, which shows the signal is already in TBM machine data. — [Bilgin & Algan, ITU](https://avesis.itu.edu.tr/yayin/ba0870b7-c9cd-4f25-8bb0-8d45165825e9/the-performance-of-a-tbm-in-a-squeezing-ground-at-uluabat-turkey)

**Cutter maintenance and hyperbaric interventions (chronic downtime and safety)**
- NeTTUN partners (EU project, about 2013-2015) put a 1-hour excavation stop at €2,500-10,000, before penalty clauses. — [IAARC/ISARC 2015, Camus & Moubarak](https://www.iaarc.org/publications/fulltext/FFACE-ISARC15-3001655.pdf)
- Replacing one disc cutter takes about 1 hour under hyperbaric conditions. A study of 50 rock-TBM projects found an average cutter change time of about 66 minutes. — [search summary of hyperbaric paper](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10786425/v1); [Tunnelling & Underground Space Technology 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0886779821001917)
- Disc cutters can weigh more than 200 kg each and have traditionally been changed by hyperbaric diving teams with hoists. — [TunnelTalk 2013](https://www.tunneltalk.com/Research-and-Development-14May2013-Robotised-maintenance-of-TBM-cutting-tools.php)
- On a Cairo Metro drive, higher fines content raised intervention frequency through clogging. A London-area drive planned for weekly interventions. — [Research Square](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10786425/v1); [World Construction Network, C320](https://www.worldconstructionnetwork.com/analysis/lessons-from-c320-s-tbm-interventions/)
- Hong Kong used saturation diving for TBM interventions, justified by the cost of downtime. — [HKIE Transactions](https://hkie.org.hk/hkietransactions/upload/2021-04-15/THIE-2019-0044.pdf)

**Worker safety and occupational health**
- Regulators rarely publish tunnelling-specific accident statistics. BLS does not separate tunnel deaths: 1,032 US construction and extraction workers died in 2024, tunnel deaths included. — [Tunnels & Tunnelling](https://tunnelsandtunnelling.com/?p=1005); [Mining Technology](https://www.mining-technology.com/sponsored/tunnelling-safety-in-the-usa-how-to-keep-workers-safe/)
- Chicago's TARP tunnel program reached 14 deaths since 1975 after a worker was crushed between a muck car and equipment. — [Mining Technology](https://www.mining-technology.com/?p=84162)
- Norwegian TBM workers (Leite et al. 2023) were all exposed to respirable crystalline silica (RCS), with geometric means of 35-413 µg/m³ by job. Workers in front of the TBM had the highest exposure. — [STAMI](https://stami.no/publikasjon/exposure-to-particulate-matter-and-respirable-crystalline-silica-in-tunnel-construction-workers-using-tunnel-boring-machines/)
- On Crossrail (Galea et al. 2016), the geometric-mean respirable dust during TBM work was 0.54 mg/m³ and elemental carbon (a diesel marker) for TBM operators was 18 µg/m³. Reducing supply air on two days visibly raised dust and silica. — [Crossrail Learning Legacy / IOM](https://learninglegacy.crossrail.co.uk/documents/occupational-exposure-respirable-dust-respirable-crystalline-silica-diesel-engine-exhaust-emissions-london-tunnelling-environment/)

### Inferences
- Ranked by cost impact, the biggest problems are unforeseen ground or obstructions (Bertha), urban ground loss and sinkholes (third-party damage, deaths, stoppages), and TBM entrapment or long stoppages. Cutter and hyperbaric downtime is a smaller but chronic cost. At €2.5-10k per hour, weekly multi-hour interventions over a 1-2 year drive add up to millions.
- Megaproject overruns such as HS2 are mainly governance and estimation problems. Sensors and computer vision only address the ground-risk and productivity slice.
- A recurring pattern is that the data exists (TBM thrust, face pressure, surface water seepage) but is not interpreted or acted on in time. Anomaly detection and alerting is a plausible role for software.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable tunnel-only global fatality statistic. ITA-AITES and HSE did not surface tunnel-specific numbers.
- I found no confirmed total Crossrail overrun figure in fetched sources. Sources gave conflicting totals (£14.8bn, more than £18bn), so I omitted it.
- I found no consolidated dollar cost for a TBM entrapment event.
- I could not determine the share of total TBM time lost to cutter changes from any single source, because it varies widely by geology.

## What sensing exists (seismic look-ahead, TBM data logging, laser scanning, photogrammetry, CV face mapping, robotic cutter changing), and what are its gaps?

### Takeaway
Every problem area has a sensing technology on the market. The common gaps are that it is point-based, intrusive, slow, interpreted by experts, or locked into a vendor's platform. Computer vision is mature in research (face classification, lining cracks, muck sizing) but thin in deployed, vendor-neutral products. Robotic cutter changing became real in 2026 at large diameters, with vision built in.

### Cited Findings
**1. Unknown ground ahead of the face (faults, voids, water, boulders)**
- Tunnel seismic prediction (TSP) typically sees about 150 m ahead, and Amberg claims 200 m or more. Like horizontal seismic profiling, it needs intrusive sensor deployment, which makes it less efficient and more expensive. Accuracy on TBM sites is generally worse than in drill-and-blast because of the lower effective signal spectrum. — [preprints.org review](https://preprints.org/manuscript/202408.2260/v1/download); [ISSMGE paper](https://www.issmge.org/uploads/publications/1/141/455.pdf)
- Seismic look-ahead "often grapple[s] with data uncertainty", so engineers question its reliability. — [Bayesian inference on continuous seismic data, 2024](https://aminer.org/pub/65f96ff013fb2c6cf6cbfb41/quantifying-tunneling-risks-ahead-of-tbm-using-bayesian-inference-on-continuous-seismic)
- BEAM is a focused induced-polarization electrical method, not seismic. A Chinese patent reports that its setup is complex and slow and its results were unsatisfactory in several Chinese tunnels. — [USPTO patent 9091779](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9091779)
- Research trend: passive look-ahead using vibrations from the working TBM as the source, with Bayesian risk estimates. — [Bohrium](https://www.bohrium.com/en/paper-details/real-time-tunneling-risk-forecasting-using-vibrations-from-the-working-tbm/868797399017979905-3783)
- A TU Graz paper warns against naive ML that time-shifts TBM operating data to "predict" ahead: "On the pointlessness of machine learning based time delayed prediction of TBM operational data". — [TU Graz](https://graz.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/on-the-pointlessness-of-machine-learning-based-time-delayed-prediction-of-tbm-operational-data(8fd11789-7956-44f2-ae9a-0a695676f865).html)
- Commercial: GEPRODE's geoRISCO uses AI/ML on instrumentation plus TBM parameters for real-time geological prediction, piloted with ACCIONA. — [Acciona imnovation](https://imnovation.acciona.com/en/?p=41185); [Taylor & Francis WTC](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/oa-edit/10.1201/9781003559047-126/georisco-predicting-geological-risks-site-tunnels-collado-bermejo-calle-vaquerizo-mendi-fern%C3%A1ndez-de-abrisqueta-rangel)

**2. TBM cutter wear inspection and changing**
- Existing sensing includes Robbins instrumented "smart" disc cutter monitoring, eddy-current, ultrasonic, magnetic-force and vibration-plus-ML methods. A 2022 Sensors paper notes that few studies use vibration for cutter wear diagnosis. — [Robbins RETC 2017](https://www.robbinstbm.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Mosavat_CutterMonitoring_RETC2017.pdf); [Sensors 2022 PMC9459918](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9459918/); [Machines 2023, magnetic force](https://mdpi.com/2075-1702/11/3/388)
- A 2024 study compared wear measurement methods, including close-range photogrammetry of a worn double disc cutter, and concluded that combining methods is best. This was offline inspection, not continuous monitoring. — [Applied Sciences 2024](https://doaj.org/article/c089951b25fd432cb3149af57d3168cf)
- Bouygues/Dragages "Telemach" robot arm on the Tuen Mun-Chek Lap Kok project (Hong Kong, about 2015-2016, Herrenknecht 17.6 m TBM) changes or cleans discs at about 7 bar. It is remote-operated with cameras from an atmospheric control room. Humans were still involved, using pressurized shuttles. — [NFF, Lorant & Combe](https://nff.no/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2020/04/21-Lorant-and-Combe.pdf); [Global Highways](https://d10dev.globalhighways.com/wh10/feature/tuen-mun-chek-lap-kok-tunnel-project?page=15)
- The EU NeTTUN project built a compact hydraulic arm behind the bulkhead because an off-the-shelf robot would have needed to be "absolutely huge". — [TunnelTalk 2015](https://tunneltalk.com/Research-and-development-10Nov2015-European-NeTTUN-programme-of-TBM-innovations-approaching-readiness-for-real-life-testing.php)
- June 2026, Xiamen-Kinmen Bridge project: China's first heavy-duty intelligent cutter-changing robot. It has 11 joints, a 500 kg payload, and covers 10-18 m cutterheads. It uses 3D infrared machine vision in the dark, damp chamber to locate each cutter and assess wear, with positioning accuracy within 1 mm. It changes a cutter in 25-40 minutes (over 50% productivity gain) and removes workers from hyperbaric entry. The maker was not named. — [China Daily, 1 Jul 2026](https://regional.chinadaily.com.cn/XMFTZ/2026-07/01/c_1194121.htm)
- "Accessible" atmospheric cutter-exchange designs, such as CRCHI's 16.07 m "Jinghua", avoid hyperbaric work through mechanical design rather than robots. — [NBMCW](https://nbmcw.com/article-report/infrastructure-construction/tunneling/crchi-mega-slurry-tbm-jinghua-assists-the-first-tunnel-section-breakthrough-of-beijing-east-sixth-ring-road-reconstruction-project.html)

**3. Face collapse, squeezing, and TBM operating data**
- Herrenknecht runs its own IIoT platform drawing on 5,300 projects. It is web-based (Herrenknecht.Connected, with VMT), and its AI flags anomalies on selected TBMs. This is a vendor-locked platform. — [Tunnel-Online](https://www.tunnel-online.info/en/artikel/automation-digitalization-and-continuous-advance-with-tunnel-boring-machines-4128444.html); [VMT](https://www.vmt-gmbh.de/en/products/analyse-information/herrenknecht-connected/)
- Research: TBM face rock-mass classification from cutterhead vibration converted to time-frequency images, using a CNN (Scientific Reports 2025). — [PMC11971402](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11971402)

**4. Surface settlement and building damage**
- Traditional monitoring relies on costly in-situ installations, so each structure gets few observations. MT-InSAR gives denser and cheaper building observations. A COSMO-SkyMed study (2011-2015) assessed damage for 858 buildings along 8 km of Crossrail. — [Bath / TU Delft](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/integrated-insar-monitoring-and-structural-assessment-of-tunnelli/); [TU Delft repo](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:d7a31f52-c3af-40c6-bd07-cccb3630e0dc)
- Sixense, a vendor, says Crossrail was the first construction project to actively use InSAR to reduce monitoring cost, which allowed earlier decommissioning of ground instruments. — [Sixense](https://satellite.sixense-group.com/?p=96812)
- GEODATA Kronos is a web-based platform that integrates instrumentation and monitoring data with alarms. It is a data platform, not AI prediction. — [Indian Infrastructure](https://indianinfrastructure.com/2020/08/10/geodatas-kronos-and-eupalinos-data-management-systems-ideal-for-tunnel-construction-and-monitoring/)

**5. NATM / drill-and-blast face mapping and geological logging**
- Chen et al. 2021 built a database of 42,400 face images from a Chinese highway tunnel and trained Inception-ResNet-V2 to classify 5 rock-structure types. — [Geoscience Frontiers 2021](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021GeoFr..12..395C/abstract)
- Kim & Yun 2024 used CNN regression to predict RMR from whole face images, with ensembling and a mobile field app. — [Engineering Geology 2024](https://x-mol.com/paper/1814028123637354496)
- NGI (Hansen et al.) applied CNNs to measure-while-drilling (MWD) drill-rig data rendered as images. They report balanced accuracy of 0.82 for binary classification in the journal version, and 0.95 binary / 0.86 multi-class Q-classes in the arXiv version. The two versions disagree. A tabular ensemble outperformed the CNNs. — [Applied Computing & Geosciences 2024](https://doaj.org/article/4e08b824ffce42a0ba4600ebd843d2c2); [arXiv 2403.10404](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.10404v1.pdf)
- Laser scanning lets discontinuity data be synthesized into a much more representative rock-mass picture than hand mapping. It also lets geologists measure without approaching the unsupported face. — [Fekete, Diederichs & Lato](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/908037742); [Greek highway TLS, Sensors](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3472883/)

**6. Shotcrete thickness and quality**
- Traditional depth pins and drilled checks give only point data. Laser scanning and photogrammetry give full thickness profiles. Rock-mass convergence between scans causes over-calculation, which needs correction. — [ACG UWA, Loncaric et al.](https://papers.acg.uwa.edu.au/p/902_09_Loncaric/); [Structurae](https://structurae.net/fr/litterature/article-de-revue/mapping-shotcrete-thickness-using-lidar-and-photogrammetry-data-correcting-for-over-calculation-due-to-rockmass-convergence)
- Normet SmartScan (Rogfast, Norway) scans before and after spraying to produce georeferenced thickness maps. These are vendor claims. — [Mining Technology (sponsored)](https://www.mining-technology.com/sponsored/concrete-spraying-the-imperative-of-data-capture-and-measurement-systems/)

**7. Lining crack and segment defect inspection**
- Tunnel defect datasets are scarce and often private. Public examples include Xue et al. 2020 (shield-tunnel leakage), Feng et al. 2024 (Shanghai metro: leakage, spalling, cracks) and TACK Tunnel Data (arXiv Dec 2025, three lining types). — [arXiv 2512.14477](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.14477v1)
- Published methods include CrackRecNet (VGG-19 in a U-Net) on the TCSC dataset from a double-shield TBM tunnel, PCNet hybrid attention for shield-tunnel cracks, and a Mask R-CNN on 761 images with 1,171 crack instances. — [Frontiers of Structural & Civil Eng 2024](https://journal.hep.com.cn/fsce/EN/10.1007/s11709-024-1048-4); [JRMGE 2023](https://doaj.org/article/a3e6a282b7fe4ba2a8f5fee819ee4212)

**8. Muck and spoil characterization on conveyors**
- Muck fragment size and shape directly reflect rock-cutter interaction and rock mass. Sieving is time-consuming and impractical in TBM tunnelling. Methods have moved from wavelets and Voronoi segmentation to CNNs and Mask R-CNN. Dust, mist and belt speed blur chip boundaries. — [MDPI Symmetry 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-8994/16/2/222/html); [Rock Mech Rock Eng 2017](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/doi/10.1007/s00603-017-1256-5)
- Rispoli et al. defined a "coarseness index" from photographs of TBM debris and related it to specific energy and RMR. — [PoliTO](https://iris.polito.it/retrieve/handle/11583/3009842/979773)
- For EPB soft ground, rule-based image analysis can distinguish sands from gravels. — [Duffner et al., WTC](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/oa-edit/10.1201/9781003559047-343/derivation-physical-properties-epb-tunneling-excavation-material-based-digital-image-processing-methods-duffner-kasselr-heim-thienert-m%C3%BCller)
- Commercial: Hexagon Split sells conveyor cameras for particle size, shape and colour, aimed at mining. — [Hexagon Split brochure](https://bynder.hexagon.com/asset/255eef35-f4f6-43bd-b18e-af09136df7b0/Hexagon_Split_Brochure_EN.pdf)

**9. Ventilation, gas, dust, and worker tracking**
- Litum UWB RTLS on Grand Paris Express uses 48 anchors in the tunnel and claims about 50 cm accuracy. It alerts on restricted-zone entry and on drilling equipment coming within 100 m of workers. These are vendor claims. — [IoT ONE case study](https://v2.iotone.com/case-study/worker-tracking-safety-monitoring-in-construction/c1208); [ForConstructionPros](https://www.forconstructionpros.com/business/construction-safety/press-release/21114683/employee-safety-technology-reduces-accidents-in-paris-metro)
- A low-cost UWB + LoRa proximity-alert prototype worked in simulated tunnels, but alerts lagged 5-8 s and positions were slightly off. — [Buildings 2025, MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/15/15/2667)
- In mining, a cap lamp combines multi-gas sensing (CH4, O2, CO) with UWB positioning at ≤30 cm error. — [Sensors 2024, PMC10857590](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10857590)

### Inferences
- Look-ahead: physics-based methods (TSP, BEAM) are intermittent, intrusive and interpreted by experts. TBM-data ML is vendor-locked or methodologically shaky, per TU Graz. Fusing continuous passive sensing with muck imagery is under-served.
- Cutter changing: by 2026 large-diameter robots with built-in machine vision exist (Xiamen, Telemach), so a startup should not try to build robots. The open niche is retrofit wear sensing and inspection for the large installed base of small and medium TBMs.
- Computer-vision research for face mapping, cracks and muck is abundant but mostly single-site Chinese or Korean datasets with weak generalization. Data scarcity, not model architecture, is the bottleneck.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm a Herrenknecht-branded cutter-change robot, or which company made the June 2026 Xiamen robot.
- I found no Amberg-specific product details in the results. Amberg Navigation and Profiler (TMS) are known products, but I have no fetched source for them.
- I found no deployed commercial computer-vision product for face mapping in NATM. Research apps exist (Kim & Yun mobile app), but adoption data does not.
- I found no camera-based PPE or intrusion AI deployments specific to tunnels.
- I found no quantified gas-incident statistics for tunnels.

## Where are the clearest openings for camera- or sensor-based AI?

### Takeaway
The clearest openings for a small team are where the input is a cheap camera or phone and the output replaces slow manual work. These are (a) face photo to rock-mass class or RMR support for the NATM geologist, (b) conveyor muck imaging as a continuous "geology ahead or at the face" proxy for TBMs, (c) lining and segment defect detection from video or phone images, and (d) before-and-after scan differencing for shotcrete thickness and overbreak. Look-ahead prediction, settlement forecasting and robotic cutter changing are high-value but hard for hackathon teams, because they need proprietary data or heavy hardware.

### Cited Findings
- Face-image datasets at scale (42,400 images) and CNN-to-RMR mobile apps are already shown in research, which proves the task is learnable. — [Geoscience Frontiers 2021](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021GeoFr..12..395C/abstract); [Engineering Geology 2024](https://x-mol.com/paper/1814028123637354496)
- Muck imaging is cheap: a camera over the belt avoids sieving, and coarseness relates to specific energy and RMR. The remaining difficulties are dust, mist and overlapping chips. — [MDPI Symmetry 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-8994/16/2/222/html); [PoliTO](https://iris.polito.it/retrieve/handle/11583/3009842/979773)
- Public lining-defect datasets now exist (TACK TTD, Shanghai metro), which lowers the data barrier for crack, leak and spall detection. — [arXiv 2512.14477](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.14477v1)
- Shotcrete point-check methods remain standard, and scan differencing gives full maps but must correct for convergence. — [ACG UWA](https://papers.acg.uwa.edu.au/p/902_09_Loncaric/)
- InSAR is cheaper and denser than in-situ settlement monitoring, and Crossrail spent up to £200M on conventional instrumentation. — [Bath/TU Delft](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/integrated-insar-monitoring-and-structural-assessment-of-tunnelli/); [IET](https://www.theiet.org/media/9513/crossrail-improving-performance-of-delivering-infrastructure.pdf)
- Vision for cutter localization and wear in the dark, wet chamber is now core to the newest cutter-change robot, with 1 mm positioning. — [China Daily 2026](https://regional.chinadaily.com.cn/XMFTZ/2026-07/01/c_1194121.htm)
- Respirable silica peaks at the TBM front and responds visibly to ventilation airflow. That suggests sensor-driven ventilation control and alerts are valuable. — [STAMI 2023](https://stami.no/publikasjon/exposure-to-particulate-matter-and-respirable-crystalline-silica-in-tunnel-construction-workers-using-tunnel-boring-machines/); [Crossrail IOM](https://learninglegacy.crossrail.co.uk/documents/occupational-exposure-respirable-dust-respirable-crystalline-silica-diesel-engine-exhaust-emissions-london-tunnelling-environment/)

### Inferences
Per-problem summary. Feasibility ratings are my judgement for a small team (hackathon or startup).

| # | Problem | Persisted | Impact (sourced above) | Why unsolved | CV / sensor approach | Existing solutions and gaps | Feasibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unknown ground and obstructions ahead of the face | Since tunnelling began; Bertha 2013 | 867-day delay, $57.2M award, about $200M over budget (Bertha) | Look-ahead is intermittent, intrusive, expert-read; TBM blocks the view | Fuse muck imagery, cutterhead vibration and TBM data; anomaly alerts; GPR or utility-record CV for urban obstructions | TSP, BEAM, geoRISCO, Herrenknecht AI; gaps: reliability, vendor lock-in | Low-Medium (needs real TBM data) |
| 2 | Cutter wear and hyperbaric changes | Decades; robots since about 2015 | €2.5-10k/hr per stop; about 1 h per cutter (hyperbaric); >200 kg discs | Hostile chamber (dark, wet, pressurized); retrofit is hard | Camera or 3D vision wear gauge during inspections; vibration ML | Telemach, 2026 Xiamen robot (large dia.), Robbins smart cutters; gap: small and medium TBMs, retrofit | Medium (photogrammetric wear gauge from photos) |
| 3 | Face collapse and squeezing / entrapment | Long-standing | Sinkholes with fatalities (Seoul 2025), city disruptions (Bangkok 2025) | Signals exist but go unread; thresholds are site-specific | Real-time anomaly detection on face pressure, thrust and muck weight versus volume; CCTV of surface seepage | Vendor platforms, Kronos alarms; gap: interpretation and response | Medium (with public or synthetic data) |
| 4 | Surface settlement and building damage | Long-standing | Crossrail: £200M I&M, 16,000 assets | Point instruments are sparse and costly | InSAR plus building-crack CV from street-level imagery; drone photogrammetry | Sixense InSAR, Kronos; gap: per-building automated risk | Medium-High (public Sentinel-1 data plus façade-crack CV) |
| 5 | NATM face mapping and geological logging | Manual mapping is the norm | Safety exposure at unsupported face; subjective classification | Data scarcity, generalization across sites | Phone or tablet photo to joint sets, RMR and Q suggestion; 3D photogrammetry of face | Research apps (Kim & Yun), MWD CNN (NGI); gap: commercial, multi-site | High (hackathon demo feasible) |
| 6 | Shotcrete thickness and quality | Point checks remain standard | Over- and under-spraying cost; under-thickness is a safety risk | Convergence errors; scanner cost | Phone LiDAR or photogrammetry before and after difference; rebound and coverage CV | Normet SmartScan; gap: low-cost, vendor-neutral | Medium-High |
| 7 | Lining crack and segment defects | Ongoing for operating tunnels | Leakage, spalling; long inspection outages | Private, site-specific datasets | Video-based crack, leak and spall segmentation; segment joint and step measurement | Research models; public datasets (TTD, Shanghai); gap: deployment | High |
| 8 | Muck characterization on conveyors | Research since the 2010s | Proxy for geology and cutter efficiency | Dust, mist, overlap; little lithology ID | Belt camera: size distribution, coarseness index, colour or lithology change detection | Hexagon Split (mining); gap: TBM-specific, lithology | High (camera plus synthetic or public rock images) |
| 9 | Ventilation, dust and gas | Long-standing | RCS 35-413 µg/m³ GM at Norwegian TBMs | Sparse sensors; manual ventilation tuning | Low-cost PM and gas sensor mesh; airflow optimization; CV for dust plumes | Mining cap lamps; gap: tunnel-specific closed loop | Medium-High (hardware kit) |
| 10 | Worker tracking and proximity | Long-standing | Crush incidents (Chicago TARP: 14 deaths since 1975) | GPS-denied; UWB infrastructure cost | Camera person-detection near muck cars or locos; UWB proximity | Litum UWB (Grand Paris); gap: camera-based, no-badge | High (YOLO-style detection demo) |
| 11 | Megaproject cost and schedule overruns | Persistent (Flyvbjerg 2003 onward) | Fixed links +34%; mega-tunnels +37%; HS2 about double | Estimation and governance failures | Automated progress tracking (ring count and advance from video), as-built vs plan | General construction CV; gap: TBM-specific | Medium (ring-counting from video is easy, value unproven) |

- Best hackathon picks are #5 (face photo to geology), #7 (lining defects, with public datasets), #8 (muck camera) and #10 (camera proximity safety). All can be demoed with a laptop, a camera and public or synthetic data.
- Best startup wedges are #2 (retrofit vision wear-gauging for the installed TBM base) and #4 (InSAR plus façade-CV building-risk service). Both have clear payers (contractors and insurers) and quantified costs.
- Avoid as a small team: robotic cutter changers (heavy hardware, incumbents arrived in 2026) and true geophysical look-ahead (physics-heavy, needs access to proprietary TBM data).

### Gaps
- Labelled image data from real tunnel faces and cutter chambers is rarely public. Feasibility for #2 and #5 depends on getting contractor data.
- I found no market-size or willingness-to-pay data for these products.
- I found no field validation of computer-vision face-mapping accuracy against geologist consensus across multiple sites.
