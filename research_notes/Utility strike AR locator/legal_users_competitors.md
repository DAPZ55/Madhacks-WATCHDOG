# Utility Strike AR Locator: Legal, User, and Competitive Context (US-first, Wisconsin specifics)

Research date: 2026-11-14. Some sources are vendor or press-release material; flagged where relevant.

## Law and liability: Wisconsin 182.0175, mandatory 811 for homeowners, liability for an estimating app

### Takeaway
In Wisconsin, every excavator, homeowners included, must notify Diggers Hotline at least 3 working days before any digging, whatever the depth or tool. Only hand digging is allowed within 18 inches of a mark. A homeowner digging on their own residential property is exempt from the state's PSC forfeitures, but ordinary civil liability for damage is preserved. An app that estimates line locations does not change those duties. The main legal exposure is a negligence or misrepresentation claim if users rely on its estimates *instead of* calling 811, so the product must never be framed as a substitute for a locate.

### Cited Findings
**Wisconsin statute (Wis. Stat. 182.0175)**
- "Excavation" means any operation that moves or displaces earth, rock, or other material using tools, equipment, or explosives, including grading, trenching, digging, ditching, and drilling. The definition has no exemption for hand tools or homeowners. — [Wis. Stat. 182.0175](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/182/0175)
- Excavators must give the one-call system at least 3 working days' notice before nonemergency excavation. "Working days" excludes Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. — [Wis. Stat. 182.0175(1)(d), (2)(am)1](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/182/0175)
- Excavators must also plan the work, taking reasonable action to learn where facilities are and avoiding them where possible (2)(a). They must renotify if marks are destroyed or covered, if work does not start within 10 days of the scheduled date, or if work stops for more than 10 days (2)(am)4. They must notify the owner immediately if a facility may have been struck, and call 911 if gas or a hazardous liquid escapes (2)(am)7. — [Wis. Stat. 182.0175](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/182/0175)
- **Clearance rule (2)(as):** Excavators must keep an estimated 18 inches between the marking of an unexposed facility and the cutting edge of power-operated equipment, except when first breaking pavement. Once the facility is exposed, the clearance may drop to the greater of twice the equipment's control limit or 12 inches. — [Wis. Stat. 182.0175(3) page](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/182/0175/3)
- The Diggers Hotline homeowner FAQ puts the rule in plain terms: within 18 inches of a marked facility "only hand digging is allowed," and no mechanized equipment may be used. — [Diggers Hotline Homeowner FAQ](https://www.diggershotline.com/homeowner-faq)
- **Owner marking duties (2m):** Owners must mark within 3 working days of notice, and respond to planning notices within 10 days. Emergency locates are due within 24 hours. Marks must be accurate "in a reasonable manner" consistent with national standards. The APWA color code applies: red electric, yellow gas/oil, orange communications, blue water, green sewer, pink survey, white proposed excavation. — [Wis. Stat. 182.0175(2m)](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/182/0175/3)
- **Private facilities:** Owners of private transmission facilities (non-governmental, on their own land, not crossing a public right-of-way) do not have to join the one-call system. The one-call system must tell callers that private facilities are not included and must mention "propane" specifically. — [Wis. Stat. 182.0175(1)(bx), (1m)(a), (1m)(e)1](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/182/0175)
- **Penalties:**
  - Sub. (3), gas and hazardous materials: the commission may assess a forfeiture of up to $25,000 per violation against a person who knew or should have known an action violated the law. Each day is a separate violation, a single persisting violation is capped at $500,000, and a 10% surcharge goes to the one-call system.
  - Sub. (3g), other facilities: a willful and knowing violation carries a forfeiture of $2,000 per offense, with each day a separate offense.
  - **Both subs exempt a residential property owner or tenant excavating their own property, and agricultural activity.** The exemption does not cover businesses that excavate for the public.
  - Sub. (3r): intentionally removing, moving, or obliterating a marking is a misdemeanor, punishable by a fine of up to $500, up to 30 days in jail, or both. Excavators who remove marks during excavation are excluded.
  - Sub. (4): existing rights of action are preserved, so civil damage claims survive.
  - Sub. (5): facility owners may seek injunctions.
  — [Wis. Stat. 182.0175(3)–(5)](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/182/0175/3)
- Complaints may be filed by the one-call system, the affected facility owner, an excavator, a locator, or the DOT or a political subdivision. The deadline is 120 days from discovery, extendable to 1 year for good cause, and the panel may allow an education course instead of a referral. — [Wis. Stat. 182.0175(3)](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/182/0175/3)
- A 2017 act describes an earlier version of the forfeiture regime, with a cap of no more than $25,000 per violation. The current text should govern. — [2017 Wis. Act 136](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/2017/related/acts/136/20?down=1)
- The 2026 Diggers Hotline damage-prevention-council (P3) draft shows active policy work in WI. I did not read its contents. — [Diggers Hotline P3 draft, Jan 2026](https://www.diggershotline.com/assets/uploads/2026/08/P3%20draft%20-%20DPC%20-%20Jan%202026.pdf)

**Is 811 mandatory for homeowners digging small holes?**
- Diggers Hotline: notice is required even for hand tools, "and it doesn't matter how deep you are digging." The service is free. — [Diggers Hotline Homeowner FAQ](https://www.diggershotline.com/homeowner-faq)
- City of Madison: everyone, including a homeowner working in their own yard, must contact Diggers Hotline 3 working days before digging. — [City of Madison, Before You Dig](https://www.cityofmadison.com/node/20706)
- Nationally, CGA and 811 centers describe calling 811 as required by law and free. — [WRDW/InvestigateTV, Oct 9 2026](https://www.wrdw.com/2026/10/09/digging-dangers-why-underground-utility-damage-is-rise/)

**Liability precedent for apps, private locators, and disclaimers**
- *Yellowstone Log Homes v. City of Rigby* (Idaho Sup. Ct. 2023): the dig law does not explicitly give "end users" a private right of action, but it does impose a duty on the city to mark sewer lines in the right-of-way. Negligence per se and common-law negligence theories went forward against the facility owner. — [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/idaho/supreme-court-civil/2023/49562.html)
- USIC, the largest contract locator, is being sued by the Glendale homeowner whose house exploded in August 2025. Separately, USIC paid a homeowner's insurance deductible after an unmarked electric line was struck during AT&T fiber work in Ballwin, MO, causing about $20,000 in surge damage. — [WRDW/InvestigateTV, Oct 9 2026](https://www.wrdw.com/2026/10/09/digging-dangers-why-underground-utility-damage-is-rise/)
- A private locating firm in Calgary disclaimed liability for a homeowner's or contractor's failure to obtain the primary (one-call) locate. This shows private locators limit liability by contract and position themselves as a supplement to the public locate. — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/9910634/digging-disaster-costly-renovation-calgary-couple/amp)
- Consumer guidance on fault: if a utility mismarked a line, the utility pays the repair. Homeowners are told they may be held responsible for strikes on their property and should verify marks. — [Angi](https://angi.com/articles/homeowner-liable-damage-gas-line-contractor.htm)
- Even official marks are "approximate," per Miss Dig 811. Electromagnetic locator depth estimates carry about ±15–20% error. — [Miss Dig 811](https://www.missdig811.org/homeowner.html); [Projul guide](https://projul.com/blog/construction-underground-utility-locating-methods-guide)
- Locators can only mark from the as-built records they receive, which "can be notoriously unreliable." — [4M Analytics blog](https://www.4manalytics.com/blog/why-doesnt-811-locate-all-utilities)

### Inferences
- An app that tells someone where to dig based on an AI estimate invites a negligence or negligent-misrepresentation claim. It also undercuts the statute's core duty to notify 811, which is a public-policy problem. Disclaimers help, but courts discount them when the product's core promise ("rough idea of where to dig") invites exactly the reliance being disclaimed. Language like "where to dig" should be removed entirely and replaced with "where to expect lines; call 811."
- The WI residential-owner forfeiture exemption means homeowners rarely face state penalties. Their real risk is civil and physical: repair bills, injury, explosion. Fear of cost and danger is the motivator, not fines.
- Contractors (non-exempt) face up to $25k per violation (gas) and $2k per offense. They have stronger compliance incentives and are more likely to pay for documentation tools.
- Safe disclaimer pattern, inferred from how private locators behave: "Informational only. Not a locate. Does not satisfy your legal obligation to contact 811/Diggers Hotline. Always call 811 at least 3 working days before digging and hand-dig within 18 inches of marks." Pair it with UX that blocks or gates "dig here" guidance until the user confirms a ticket number.

### Gaps
- I did not read the exact current text of sub. (3)/(3g). The summary came via a page fetch, and an aggregator gave the $500,000 persisting-violation cap. Confirm against the official PDF.
- I found no case law on a consumer app (or an AI estimate) causing a strike, and no published app disclaimer language. This appears to be genuinely unprecedented territory.
- I did not verify whether Diggers Hotline itself publishes a mobile app. Its FAQ page does not mention one.

## Who is not served by 811, homeowner damage share, and why people skip 811

### Takeaway
811 marks only member-owned public facilities. Everything past the meter is unmarked unless the owner hires a private locator: sprinklers, landscape lighting, detached-garage and barn feeds, propane lines, sewer laterals, septic. Roughly 4 in 10 to half of US homeowners who plan to dig say they will not call 811, mostly because they think the job is "too shallow." Nationally, "no notification" accounts for about 22–25% of damages.

### Cited Findings
- WI Diggers Hotline: private lines "will not be marked." Examples are propane lines, lines after the meter, sewer laterals, sprinkler systems, ornamental lighting, and electric lines to a barn or garage. Homeowners can hire a private locator, and the FAQ links a locator directory. — [Diggers Hotline Homeowner FAQ](https://www.diggershotline.com/homeowner-faq)
- Texas 811's list of lines generally not marked: water and sewer lines, private lighting, sprinklers, fire mains, secondary electric to detached garages, pools, septic. — [Texas 811](https://texas811.org/what-to-expect)
- Colorado 811 and Indiana 811 do not mark private lines. Locating them is the property owner's responsibility and expense, so a private locator is needed. — [Colorado 811](https://www.colorado811.org/resources/blog/private-v-public-utilities-whats-the-difference); [Indiana 811](https://indiana811.org/?p=15110); [Gopher State One Call](https://gopherstateonecall.org/what-does-not-get-marked/)
- Propane suppliers will often mark tank lines for free if asked. This claim comes from a secondary guide of unclear provenance. — [dig811guide](https://dig811guide.pages.dev/private-lines.html)
- **CGA homeowner surveys:**
  - 2025: 46% of Americans planning a DIY dig do not plan to contact 811. The top reason was the belief that the project was too shallow; unfamiliarity with the 811 process was also cited. 68% planned a DIY dig in the next year. Fielded by SurveyMonkey among 616 US homeowners, Feb 14–15 2025, MOE ±3.95%. — [Pumper, Apr 2025](https://www.pumper.com/online_exclusives/2025/04/survey-finds-46-of-americans-plan-to-dig-without-contacting-811-beforehand); [Albany CEO/Georgia 811](https://albanyceo.com/news/2025/04/survey-finds-46-americans-plan-dig-without-contacting-811-beforehand-risking-utility-disruptions-and-safety-hazards)
  - 2026: 619 homeowners, Feb 26–27 2026. An estimated 10M+ homeowners plan to dig without contacting 811, again mainly because they think the job is too shallow. I did not find the percentage. — [Bermex/ACRT](https://bermex.acrt.com/utility-metering-news/articles/survey-more-than-60-million-american-homeowners-plan-to-dig-this-spring-heres-how-to-stay-safe-before-breaking-ground/)
  - Earlier years: 49% (2022 release), about 40%, 36%, and 48%. CGA's headline estimates ranged from 26.9M (2024) to 34.9M and 49.3M Americans planning to dig without calling. — [CGA 49.3M release](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/survey-reveals-49-3-million-americans-plan-to-dig-without-contacting-811-first-risking-disruption-to-critical-services-301784138.html); [CGA ~40% release](https://commongroundalliance.com/News/survey-reveals-nearly-40-percent-of-homeowners-who-plan-to-dig-this-year-will-put-themselves-and-others-at-risk-by-not-calling-811-before-starting); [AmeriSurv 2024, 26.9M](https://amerisurv.com/2024/03/28/survey-reveals-26-9-million-americans-plan-to-dig-without-contacting-811-first/); [Trenchless Tech, 48%](https://trenchlesstechnology.com/cga-survey-48-percent-of-homeowners-forgo-calling-811-before-beginning-a-diy-digging-project/)
- **CGA DIRT data:**
  - 2025 preview, reported Aug 2026: 221,717 unique damage reports; CGA Index 102 (2022 = 100), up from 96.7 in 2024. Root-cause shares:
    - No notification to 811: 22.3%
    - Locator error, not marked: 14.2%
    - Locator error, mismarked: 9.2%
    - Not marked because the operator or locator did not respond: 4.2%
    - Failed to maintain clearance after verifying marks: 14.8%
    - Dug before potholing to verify: 7.2%
    - Dug outside the ticket area: 3.0%
    - Dug after the ticket expired: 2.2%
    - The top 10 causes make up 86% of damages.
  - Telecom/CATV took 51% of damages. Fencing/landscaping caused 7% of telecom damages.
  — [Cabling Installation & Maintenance, Aug 6 2026](https://www.cablinginstall.com/design-install/news/55396303/underground-utility-damage-worsened-in-2025-and-telecom-networks-got-hit-the-most)
  - 2024: 196,977 unique damage reports. The industry is off track for the goal of cutting damages 50% in 5 years. — [Compact Equipment](https://compactequip.com/business/cga-releases-2024-dirt-report-findings-highlights-need-for-industry-improvements/)
  - About one in four 2024 damages happened because people did not call 811. CGA's Sarah Magruder Lyle: "Not contacting 811 is a quarter of the incidents we see." — [WRDW, Oct 2026](https://www.wrdw.com/2026/10/09/digging-dangers-why-underground-utility-damage-is-rise/)
  - 2021 DIRT: no notification was the top root cause, at more than a quarter of damages. 60% of no-notification damages were attributed to professional excavators, not homeowners. — [CGA 2021 DIRT Exec Summary](https://dirt.commongroundalliance.com/2021-DIRT-Report/Executive-Summary)

### Inferences
- The private-line gap is real, statutory (WI explicitly excludes private facilities and requires a propane warning), and unaddressed by 811. It is the clearest "not served" segment: rural and suburban WI homes with propane, detached garages, irrigation, and septic.
- Locator error (about 27.6% combined in 2025) and failure to maintain clearance or verify marks (about 22% combined) together exceed no-notification. Tools aimed at mark verification and mark persistence target a larger pool of damages than tools aimed at homeowners who skip 811.
- Most no-notification damage comes from professionals (60% in 2021), so a homeowner-only product addresses a minority of damages.

- I did not find the percentage for the 2026 CGA survey (only "10M+").
- I found no reliable data on wait-time complaints as a reason for skipping 811. "Too shallow" and "unfamiliar" are the documented reasons.
- I found no WI-specific DIRT numbers.

## Safe positioning options

### Takeaway
The lowest-risk, clearest-value angles never tell anyone where to dig. They (1) educate before the call ("expect a gas service from the street to the meter here; call 811; your sprinkler and garage lines are private and won't be marked"), (2) capture, geotag, and persist 811 paint and flag marks as AR or photo records once marks fade, with an evidence trail, and (3) route users to private locators for private lines. Approximating where lines are, an AI guess presented as dig guidance, is the highest-risk framing.

### Cited Findings
- WI law requires renotification when marks are destroyed or covered. Intentionally obliterating marks is a misdemeanor. Marks are therefore legally significant but physically fragile, which supports a mark-persistence product as an aid. It cannot replace a re-locate. — [Wis. Stat. 182.0175(2)(am)4, (3r)](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/182/0175/3)
- "Failed to maintain clearance after verifying marks" (14.8%) and "dug before verifying by potholing" (7.2%) are top DIRT causes. Operators losing track of marks is a measurable problem. — [Cabling Install, Aug 2026](https://www.cablinginstall.com/design-install/news/55396303/underground-utility-damage-worsened-in-2025-and-telecom-networks-got-hit-the-most)
- The industry already uses AI to audit locate photos. Irth's AI checks whether flag and paint colors match the ticket and whether the photo distance meets requirements. iFactory flags a missing color for a ticketed utility as a potentially incomplete locate (vendor claims). — [Irth Insights](https://irthsolutions.com/damage-prevention/insights-for-damage-prevention); [iFactory](https://ifactoryapp.com/industries/infrastructure-management/utility-excavation-safety-dig-damage-prevention-ai-811)
- CGA's advice to homeowners: "If you don't see paint on the ground, it's okay to ask." A verification and checklist UX fits that message. — [WRDW, Oct 2026](https://www.wrdw.com/2026/10/09/digging-dangers-why-underground-utility-damage-is-rise/)
- Diggers Hotline links a private-locator directory from its homeowner FAQ, a natural referral channel. — [Diggers Hotline FAQ](https://www.diggershotline.com/homeowner-faq)

### Inferences
Ranked from least to most legal risk:
1. **Pre-call education and planning** ("what will and won't be marked on my lot," a private-line checklist, an inline 811 ticket button, a 3-working-day countdown). Lowest risk because it pushes users *toward* compliance.
2. **Mark capture and persistence** (photograph or AR-anchor the 811 marks, compare them to the ticket's utility list, keep a timestamped evidence log). Low risk if labeled "record of marks as placed, not a locate." Clear value for disputes, for contractors after marks fade, and for showing operators where the marks were.
3. **Private-locator dispatch or referral marketplace.** Low to moderate risk (referral liability). Monetizable, and it fills the statutory gap.
4. **AI-estimated line location** ("rough idea where to dig"). Highest risk. If kept at all, limit it to "likely service-line corridor" visualization *gated behind a confirmed ticket*, never shown as a dig/no-dig zone.
- For a hackathon or MVP, 1 + 2 combined ("Plan, Call, Capture") is the defensible angle. The AI could recognize paint colors and anchor marks in AR rather than guess buried geometry.

### Gaps
- I found no legal commentary specifically evaluating mark-photo evidence apps or AR mark overlays.
- I found no data on how long paint marks last in practice.

## Competitors and adjacent products

### Takeaway
The space is enterprise-heavy and consolidating. Itron bought Urbint ($325M, closed late 2025) and Locusview ($525M). Exodigo raised $96M (Jul 2025). 4M Analytics sells subsurface maps. Ticket-management SaaS (UtiliSync, BOSS811, Irth, Utilocate) serves utilities and contractors. AI locate-photo auditing already exists (Irth). I found no consumer app that estimates buried-line positions for homeowners. Consumer 811 apps are ticket-scheduling tools.

### Cited Findings
- **Urbint:** AI ranking of 811 tickets for third-party damage risk. Itron announced the acquisition Oct 6 2025 for $325M cash, and it closed around Nov 2025 (about $330.7M preliminary). Itron claims utilities cut strikes by up to 35%; this is a vendor claim. urbint.com now redirects to Itron's resiliency solutions page. — [Refresh Miami](https://refreshmiami.com/news/itron-to-acquire-miami-based-urbint-in-325m-deal-to-expand-ai-powered-solutions/); [Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2764584/itron-to-acquire-urbint-in-$325m-deal-to-expand-ai-capabilities); [Itron](https://de.itron.com/w/powering-the-next-chapter-of-utility-resilience)
- **Locusview** (digital as-builts, construction management): acquired by Itron in a $525M buyout. — [Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2794237/itri-boosts-resiliency-solutions-portfolio-with-525m-locusview-buyout)
- **Exodigo:** AI, multi-sensor, 3D non-intrusive subsurface mapping. $96M Series B on Jul 16 2025, bringing the total to $214M; a reported valuation of about $700M is unconfirmed. — [Railway Technology](https://www.railway-technology.com/news/exodigo-96m-series-b-funding/); [Construction Briefing](https://www.constructionbriefing.com/software/news/underground-ai-geomapping-firm-raises-nearly-us100m/8081867.article)

- **Irth:** 811 ticket management plus AI ticket screening. Its Insights product uses AI image analysis of locate photos for paint and flag color correctness and photo distance. ### Inferences
- Enterprise incumbents own utility-side risk scoring, as-builts, and ticket management. A small team should not compete there.
- The open white space is the consumer/small-contractor layer: (a) private-line awareness and locator referral, and (b) lightweight mark capture/AR persistence for small contractors and homeowners who will not buy Irth or BOSS811.
- AI paint-mark recognition is proven enough commercially (Irth) to be credible as a feature, and it is a safer AI use than guessing buried geometry.

## Who would pay

### Takeaway
Willingness to pay is proven on the enterprise side: utilities and their vendors pay hundreds of millions in M&A value and recurring SaaS fees, and contractors pay about $300–$2,500 per user per year for ticket management. Homeowners pay ad hoc for private locates, but there is no evidence they will pay for an app. A free consumer app with locator referral or contractor upsell revenue looks more viable than consumer subscriptions.

### Cited Findings
- Itron paid $325M for Urbint and $525M for Locusview. Utilities fund damage-prevention software within resiliency budgets. — [Refresh Miami](https://refreshmiami.com/news/itron-to-acquire-miami-based-urbint-in-325m-deal-to-expand-ai-powered-solutions/); [Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2794237/itri-boosts-resiliency-solutions-portfolio-with-525m-locusview-buyout)
- Exodigo has raised about $214M or more from investors betting on paid subsurface mapping for infrastructure owners. — [Railway Technology](https://www.railway-technology.com/news/exodigo-96m-series-b-funding/)
- Contractors and utilities pay for 811 ticket software: BOSS811 from about $2,500 per user per year, Utilocate from $25 per user per month (aggregator figures). — [Capterra BOSS811](https://capterra.com/p/206223/BOSS811/); [Capterra Utilocate](https://www.capterra.com/p/249666/Utilocate/)
- Locate companies bear real liability costs (deductible payouts, explosion suits), which motivates them to buy evidence and photo-audit tools. — [WRDW, Oct 2026](https://www.wrdw.com/2026/10/09/digging-dangers-why-underground-utility-damage-is-rise/)
- Homeowners must pay for private locates themselves ("at their own expense"). 811 itself is free. — [Colorado 811](https://www.colorado811.org/resources/blog/private-v-public-utilities-whats-the-difference); [Diggers Hotline FAQ](https://www.diggershotline.com/homeowner-faq)
- Missouri now requires tracer wire or locator tape on new lines, part of a regulatory trend toward locatability that creates demand for documentation. — [WRDW, Oct 2026](https://www.wrdw.com/2026/10/09/digging-dangers-why-underground-utility-damage-is-rise/)

### Inferences
- The most plausible early payers are small excavation and landscaping/fencing contractors (mark-photo evidence for disputes, 7% of telecom damage comes from fencing/landscaping) and private locators (lead generation). Insurers and municipalities are plausible but unproven.
- Utilities would want this only as a channel to drive 811 compliance, e.g., co-branded education. This fits the 811 centers' outreach budgets more than direct sales.

### Gaps
- I found no data on homeowner willingness to pay, private-locate prices, insurer interest, or average cost per strike (the CGA societal-cost figure was not retrieved).
- I found no evidence of utility damage-prevention budget sizes.
