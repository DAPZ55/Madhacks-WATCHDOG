# Cameras can stop the digging industry's oldest killers

The best target for camera or cheap-sensor AI is **underground utility strikes**. US excavators damaged buried lines **196,977 times in 2024**, the industry's own damage index is rising, and the economic hit is modeled at **$83.2B a year**. Nothing on the market watches the bucket against the 811 paint marks while digging happens, and a phone or cab camera that reads colored marks is buildable in a weekend. Close behind are **trench cave-ins** (about 20 US deaths a year, 39 in 2022, 17 in 2025), which no commercial vision product addresses even though the OSHA rules are visual checklists, and **workers struck by machinery**, which is the top killer in US mining and a leading killer in construction, tunneling and on drill rigs. Across construction, geotech, mining, tunneling and drilling, 42 distinct problems emerged. They fall into three groups. Process-driven problems have stalled for decades even though the hazard is visible: utility strikes, trench entry, struck-by. Expert visual judgments remain subjective: core logging, soil classification, rock-face mapping, bit grading, cuttings description. Expensive vendor systems serve only tier-1 mines and megaprojects: fragmentation, tooth loss, belt rips, slope radar. For a small team, the first two groups are the opening. Many headline numbers in this space come from vendors or trade-press snippets and are flagged below.

## The ranked list: 42 digging problems, best opportunity first

**How to read this.** Each entry gives the problem, the key impact figure, the AI/sensor approach, what exists and where the gap is, and the feasibility for a hackathon or small team. Rank combines real-world impact (deaths, cost, frequency, how long it has gone unsolved) with how practical a camera or cheap-sensor build is. Feasibility ratings are judgment calls, not sourced facts. Tags: **[vendor]** means a company claim with no independent validation found. **[unverified]** means a secondary, snippet-level, single-source or extrapolated figure.

### Tier 1: high impact, buildable now (1-10)

**1. Underground utility strikes during digging** *(construction, HDD/drilling, homeowner and fence-post digging; merged)*
- **Problem:** Excavators, augers, drills and homeowners keep hitting buried gas, telecom, power and water lines after 50 years of one-call programs and 811.
- **Impact:** **196,977** US damage reports in 2024, with CGA's damage index up from 94.0 to 96.7 ([Compact Equipment](https://compactequip.com/business/cga-releases-2024-dirt-report-findings-highlights-need-for-industry-improvements/)). 2025 rose to **221,717** [unverified: trade-press snippet, page returned 403] ([Trenchless Technology](https://trenchlesstechnology.com/cga-releases-2025-dirt-data-summary-trends/)). The modeled total cost is **$83.2B/yr**, of which only $4.9B is direct repair ([E+E Leader](https://environmentenergyleader.com/stories/underground-utility-damage-creates-832b-in-economic-impact,140943)). PHMSA estimates nearly $30B/yr and counts **40 deaths and 166 serious injuries** from excavation-related pipeline incidents since 2005 ([PHMSA 2026](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-07752.pdf)). Damages where nobody called 811 were more than 25% of the 2022 total, and roughly 23% of those came from non-professionals ([Link2Build](https://link2build.ca/news/articles/2023/september/dirt-report-finds-more-excavation-related-damage-to-buried-utilities)).
- **AI/sensor approach:** A phone or cab camera segments the color-coded paint marks and flags, geo-registers them, and warns when the bucket or auger enters the tolerance zone. It also detects exposed pipe or warning tape in the cut, and stores time-stamped pre-dig photos of the marks as liability evidence. A consumer "before you dig" app version covers homeowners and fence posts.
- **Existing / gap:** Exodigo ($214M raised, per the company) maps utilities before the dig with multi-sensor AI ([Informed Infrastructure](https://informedinfrastructure.com/post/exodigo-brings-ai-efficiency-to-infrastructure-industry-closes-96-million-series-b)). Urbint, now part of Itron, scores risk on 811 tickets. EM locators cannot see plastic pipe, and GPR fails in clay ([U. Pretoria](https://issuu.com/universityofpretoria/docs/innovate_15_2020/s/11079642)). **No at-the-bucket verification tool and no consumer tool were found.**
- **Feasibility: High.** Color-mark segmentation plus a zone alert is a weekend demo. No public dataset of locate marks was found, so you will have to shoot your own.

**2. Trench collapses and cave-ins**
- **Problem:** Workers enter trenches without shoring, shields or sloping. Protective systems exist, and the failure happens at the moment of entry.
- **Impact:** About **21 deaths/yr** from 2011 to 2018 ([Equipment World / BLS](https://www.equipmentworld.com/workforce/safety/article/15293290/trench-collapses-kill-13-workers-so-far-in-2022)). A record **39 in 2022**, up 160% ([Safety+Health / CPWR](https://safetyandhealthmagazine.com/articles/25402-trenching-and-excavations-deaths-rose-160-in-2022-cpwr)). After an enforcement-driven dip, deaths rebounded to **17 in 2025** ([Confined Space](https://jordanbarab.com/confinedspace/2026/01/08/trench-collapse-deaths-ring-out-the-old-year/)). Media counts undercount, because deaths in state-plan states often go unreported.
- **AI/sensor approach:** A camera at the trench edge or a phone checks the conditions OSHA requires. It flags a worker in a trench of 5 ft or deeper with no protective system ([29 CFR 1926.652](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.652)), spoil within 2 ft of the edge, or no ladder within 25 ft ([1926.651](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.651)). It estimates depth with monocular depth or phone LiDAR, spots edge cracks, and alerts the competent person.
- **Existing / gap:** **No commercial trench-specific vision product and no trench-specific CV paper were found** in a targeted search. A short search is weak evidence that nothing exists, so verify before pitching.
- **Feasibility: High.** The story is novel and the rules map one-to-one onto visual checks.

**3. Workers struck by or caught in moving machinery** *(construction excavators and backing trucks, mine haul trucks, tunnel muck trains, rig-floor red zones; merged)*
- **Problem:** People on foot get hit in machine blind spots and swing radii, and legacy fleets have no sensing.
- **Impact:** Powered haulage was the leading US mining killer in 2025 at **13 of 33 deaths** [unverified: Pit & Quarry summary of MSHA data]. MSHA calls it "the most common cause of fatal accidents in mining" ([AIHA](https://www.aiha.org/news/250327-msha-issues-safety-alert-following-deaths-of-10-miners-in-early-2025)). Mobile equipment was the #1 hazard at ICMM member companies, with **9 of 42 deaths in 2024** ([Global Mining Review](https://www.globalminingreview.com/mining/11072025/icmm-reports-2024-safety-performance-of-members/)). Struck-by caused **804 US construction deaths** from 2011 to 2015 ([Safety+Health](https://safetyandhealthmagazine.com/articles/reducing-backover-incidents-2)). Chicago's TARP tunnel program has had 14 deaths since 1975 ([Mining Technology](https://www.mining-technology.com/?p=84162)).
- **AI/sensor approach:** Retrofit fisheye cameras run edge person detection against swing-radius or red-zone polygons and use machine state to suppress false alarms. The same camera can check whether the quick-coupler is locked, since NIOSH flags buckets that detach and strike workers ([NIOSH](https://blogs.cdc.gov/niosh-science-blog/2022/03/10/stuck-by-injuries)).
- **Existing / gap:** Cat Detect (3 cameras, 270°, 9 m range, new Cat machines only) ([Cat](https://www.cat.com/en_GB/products/new/technology/detect/detect/117380.html)). Blaxtair claims "up to 80% fewer near misses" [vendor] ([Blaxtair](https://blaxtair.com/en/blog/excavator-blind-spot-safety-systems)). H&P's rig red-zone pilot logged 154 alerts in 114 days ([H&P SPE-208764](https://www.helmerichpayne.com/media/technical-publications/A-Better-Set-of-Eyes-Computer-Vision-Technology-to-Monitor-Safety-Zones-and-Automate-Drill-Pipe-Tally-SPE-208764-MS.pdf)). Nabors, Seadrill and Rolloos sell similar systems, and Litum sells UWB tags for tunnels. **None has independent injury-reduction data.** Mixed-brand, rental and small-quarry fleets are underserved.
- **Feasibility: Medium-High.** A YOLO demo is easy. Beating incumbents on reliability and false-alarm rate is hard.

**4. Haul-truck and heavy-equipment operator fatigue**
- **Problem:** Microsleeps and distraction in long, monotonous haul cycles cause collisions.
- **Impact:** Cat's Driver Safety System captured **nearly 2 million** fatigue and distraction events, and one trial logged **42 fatigue events in a week across six trucks** ([Mining.com](https://www.mining.com/web/cat-dealers-delivering-safety-technology-to-reduce-operator-fatigue-and-distraction)). Vendors claim reductions of >94% (Seeing Machines) and 98% (Optalert) [vendor] ([Optalert](https://www.optalert.com/?p=10211)).
- **AI/sensor approach:** A commodity IR cab camera tracks face landmarks and computes PERCLOS eye closure and gaze-off-road alerts.
- **Existing / gap:** Seeing Machines Guardian, Cat DSS and Optalert. No independent study was found, and the price excludes small fleets, quarries and contractors.
- **Feasibility: High** for a demo. The trust and regulatory bar for production is high.

**5. Slope failure: landslides, pit walls, excavation walls and rockfall** *(geotech plus mining; merged)*
- **Problem:** Slopes fail with little warning. Radar is too expensive to deploy everywhere, and satellites revisit too slowly.
- **Impact:** Landslides cause about **$20B/yr** in losses and caused **more than 55,000 deaths from 2004 to 2016**, excluding quake-triggered slides ([Geoenvironmental Disasters 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40677-022-00205-6)). Fall of ground was the #2 ICMM fatality hazard in 2024.
- **AI/sensor approach:** Fixed time-lapse cameras run change detection and optical flow, with thermal cameras for night rockfall. Sub-$100 MEMS tilt nodes report over LoRa. Free Sentinel-1 InSAR screens wide areas.
- **Existing / gap:** GroundProbe and IDS slope radars claim sub-millimetre accuracy [vendor] ([GroundProbe](https://www.groundprobe.com/?p=19639)). InSAR's 6-12 day revisit is too slow for real-time warning ([MDPI Remote Sensing](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/18/7/1081)). In-situ early-warning systems cost too much to put on every slope ([Eng. Geology 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0013795224003715)). A University of Arizona prototype detects rockfall with an **~US$8K thermal camera** ([UA ARMA 2024](https://mining.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/2024-08/2024_Development%20of%20a%20Prototype%20Thermal%20Imaging%20Rockfall%20Detection%20System_Potter_ARMA.pdf)). The cheap middle tier between radar and InSAR is open.
- **Feasibility: Medium.** Hardware, weather and false alarms add risk.

**6. Unforeseen ground conditions and obstructions** *(construction "differing site conditions", geotech site investigation, tunnel look-ahead; merged)*
- **Problem:** Boreholes sample a sliver of the ground, so contractors dig into unexpected soil, rock, water or buried objects, and the claims follow.
- **Impact:** US highways spend about **1% of contract on site investigation and see about 3% in geotechnical overruns** ([NCHRP via NAP](https://www.nap.edu/read/21926/chapter/2)). Unforeseen physical conditions hit **18.5% of US and 29.6% of Canadian projects** [unverified: snippet of HKA CRUX] ([HKA](https://www.hka.com/article/designs-on-better-outcomes-getting-to-the-crux-of-time-and-cost-overruns-on-capital-projects/)). Tunnel claims ran at about 12% of construction cost in a 1995 study of 87 tunnels ([Boutsis et al. 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2412-3811/5/9/73)). Seattle's Bertha TBM hit an unmapped well casing, which led to an **867-day delay** and a $57.2M award to WSDOT ([Equipment World](https://www.equipmentworld.com/better-roads/article/14971884/wsdot-wins-572m-suit-over-berthas-breakdowns-during-alaskan-way-viaduct-project)).
- **AI/sensor approach:** (a) Cameras log the strata actually exposed in a trench, test pit or face and compare them with the borehole model, which surfaces differing-site-conditions evidence early. (b) OCR digitizes legacy PDF boring logs and feeds probabilistic 3D stratigraphy with uncertainty bands. (c) For TBMs, muck imagery is fused with cutterhead vibration and machine data.
- **Existing / gap:** Seequent Leapfrog does modeling. The ML stratigraphy research consists mostly of preprints benchmarked on simulated sites. Tunnel seismic look-ahead requires intrusive setup and its reliability is questioned ([preprints.org](https://preprints.org/manuscript/202408.2260/v1/download)). Herrenknecht's AI is tied to its own platform. A counter-view holds that "unforeseen ground" often hides plain underestimation ([arXiv 1901.11123](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.11123)).
- **Feasibility: Medium** for (a) and (b). **Low** for (c), which needs proprietary TBM data.

**7. Field soil classification (USCS visual-manual and OSHA soil type)**
- **Problem:** Engineers and competent persons classify soil by eye and feel. The call is subjective, and in a trench it decides how steep the walls can be and whether shoring is required.
- **Impact:** ASTM D2488 says precision "is dependent on the competence of the personnel" ([ASTM D2488-26](https://store.astm.org/d2488-26.html)). No published field-versus-lab misclassification rate was found, so the impact runs indirectly through entries 2 and 6.
- **AI/sensor approach:** A phone photo plus guided manual tests (dilatancy, toughness, dry strength), optionally with a moisture probe or pocket penetrometer, produces a suggested USCS group or OSHA Type A/B/C and groups samples for lab testing.
- **Existing / gap:** Phone CNNs classify agronomic soil classes, for example Soil-MobiNet at about 93% on 9 Indian soil types ([MDPI Sensors](https://mdpi.com/1424-8220/23/15/6709)). A smartphone texture model reports R² of 0.98-0.99 [validation design not checked] ([Agronomy 2022](https://openaccess.library.uitm.edu.my/find/Record/doaj-art-7999737dc36c4c81859244f7c55bece1)). **No model uses USCS or OSHA classes.**
- **Feasibility: High** for a demo. The moat is labeled photos tied to D2487 lab results.

**8. Landmines and explosive remnants of war**
- **Problem:** Clearing huge, often vegetated areas is slow, and farmers and children are the casualties.
- **Impact:** At least **6,279 casualties in 2024**, the highest since 2020. 90% were civilians and 46% of civilian casualties were children ([Landmine Monitor 2025](https://the-monitor.org/api/assets/reports/Landmine-Monitors/LMM2025/Downloads/LM2025-Major-Findings_EN.pdf)). Victim-assistance funding fell 23% ([NPA](https://www.npaid.org/mine-action-and-disarmament/news/new-data-shows-thousands-of-civilians-killed-and-injured-by-landmines-and-explosive-remnants-of-war)).
- **AI/sensor approach:** Drone RGB, optionally with thermal, runs a detector that flags surface ordnance for human survey teams.
- **Existing / gap:** Safe Pro AI's SpotlightAI claims about 90% detection in optimal conditions [vendor] and struggles with buried or vegetated mines ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/clear-land-mines-drones-ai-2668391125)). A public RGB+LWIR dataset of 12,078 images exists ([arXiv 2512.20487](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.20487v1)).
- **Feasibility: Medium.** The demo works on public data. Fielding it requires demining partners, and missed detections are lethal.

**9. Drill-core logging (RQD, fractures, lithology)** *(mining plus civil site investigation; merged)*
- **Problem:** Geologists log core by hand. Results vary with experience, and drilling-induced breaks logged as natural fractures push RQD down.
- **Impact:** Logging is "inherently subjective" ([Datarock](https://help.datarock.com.au/knowledge-base/how-to-qa-qc-predicted-and-manually-logged-geotech-data)). Logger expertise ranges "from low to very high" ([ACG Morrell](https://papers.acg.uwa.edu.au/d/2335_36_Morrell/36_Morrell.pdf)). Datarock claims 30,000 m of RQD in about 15 minutes [vendor] ([Datarock](https://datarock.com.au/)).
- **AI/sensor approach:** Phone photos of core trays go through SAM-style segmentation. The output is per-run RQD, recovery, fracture count and a mechanical-versus-natural break flag, exported to AGS/DIGGS logs.
- **Existing / gap:** Datarock, Minalyze, Corescan (Epiroc) and Veracio all target mining and scanner hardware. **Small civil and geotech jobs are largely unserved.** No independent accuracy benchmark was found.
- **Feasibility: High.** The work is pure image ML on ordinary photos.

**10. Rock fragment sizing** *(post-blast fragmentation, TBM muck on conveyors, oversize on belts; merged)*
- **Problem:** Fragment size drives crusher and mill energy and is a proxy for ground conditions, but sieving is impractical and image tools still miss fines.
- **Impact:** Image sizing dates to WipFrag in 1986, and **fines estimation is still the major error source** ([Bamford et al. 2021](https://www.dynsyslab.org/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/bamford-ijrmms21.pdf)). TBM muck coarseness tracks specific energy and RMR ([PoliTO](https://iris.polito.it/retrieve/handle/11583/3009842/979773)). The downstream energy cost was not quantified in these notes.
- **AI/sensor approach:** A phone, drone or belt camera feeds SAM segmentation, which produces a particle-size curve (P50/P80), a coarseness index, and alerts on oversize or lithology changes.
- **Existing / gap:** WipFrag, Split-Desktop, Orica FRAGTrack and Hexagon Split. A SAM-based method **beat Split-Desktop 4.0** on test data ([SAM paper](https://bohrium.dp.tech/paper/arxiv/0e4f5bd9ffbbd1ba2c75117b539342a57e42c4c3f6e92395c7cfb88291a7fba2)). Public datasets exist on [Roboflow](https://roboflow.com/ai/rock-fragmentation-analysis).
- **Feasibility: High.**

## Visible hazards with stalled processes beat hidden ones with better physics

The ranking favors a clear pattern: **the most useful targets are hazards a camera can already see but nobody is watching**. Utility strikes and trench deaths have persisted not because the danger is invisible but because the existing safeguards depend on humans doing the right thing at the right moment. One-call programs have existed since the 1970s, yet CGA's damage index moved the wrong way from 2023 to 2024, and **the top 10 root causes still account for 85-86% of damages** ([Compact Equipment](https://compactequip.com/business/cga-releases-2024-dirt-report-findings-highlights-need-for-industry-improvements/)). Trench deaths track enforcement intensity rather than technology. OSHA credited its National Emphasis Program for the fall to 15 deaths in 2023 ([DOL](https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/releases/osha/osha20241104-0)), and the rebound to 17 in 2025 shows how fragile that gain is. When the process has plateaued and the failure is visible, continuous visual monitoring is the obvious next layer.

The opposite case explains why several high-cost problems rank lower. Subsurface sensing runs into hard physics limits. EM locators cannot detect plastic pipe, GPR depth readings ran consistently shallow in field trials ([U. Pretoria](https://issuu.com/universityofpretoria/docs/innovate_15_2020/s/11079642)), and tunnel seismic look-ahead requires intrusive setup and still faces doubts about reliability. Kicks, lost circulation, buried mines, deep UXO and graves all need GPR, magnetometers or flow sensors, and capital-heavy players already compete there. Exodigo has raised $214M for pre-dig mapping alone. A small team gains more by owning the moment of excavation than by trying to out-sense the ground.

## Expert eyeballing and tier-1-only hardware leave the second-largest opening

The second cluster consists of **standardized visual judgments that humans make inconsistently**: RQD from core, USCS soil groups, joint sets on a rock face, IADC bit dull codes, cuttings lithology. Every one has published evidence of subjectivity. Six operators interpreting the same lineaments disagreed ([Solid Earth](https://se.copernicus.org/preprints/se-2018-135/se-2018-135-AC3-supplement.pdf)). Two evaluators can grade the same bit differently. Even core-logging vendors call the work inherently subjective. The output of each judgment plugs into a document that already exists, such as a boring log, an RMR sheet or a bit record, so a tool can slot into the current workflow instead of creating a new one. Foundation models have lowered the modeling barrier. SAM-based segmentation already beat a commercial fragmentation package on test data, and public datasets now exist for fragmentation, belt rips and tunnel lining defects.

The mining entries (tooth loss, belt rips, fragmentation, fatigue, slope radar) share a different gap. **Working products exist, but they are single-purpose hardware sold to tier-1 operators**, often bundled with consumables, as Weir does with its teeth. Quarries, mid-tier mines and artisanal miners are left out. ASM may account for the largest uncounted death toll in all of digging, and it has essentially no sensing at all. A cheap retrofit can still find a market here even though the problems are "solved" at the top of the industry.

## The most-quoted numbers are the least reliable

A builder pitching any of these should know which figures will hold up under scrutiny. The two national utility-damage cost estimates differ by almost 3x: CGA models $83.2B, while PHMSA estimates about $30B. The CGA model also adjusts for unreported damages, and DIRT reporting is voluntary. Drilling NPT estimates differ by nearly an order of magnitude, from about $1B to $8B a year, because definitions vary and the higher figure dates from 2004. The ASM toll of roughly 30,000 deaths a year is an extrapolation in a field where Delve states there is no way to validate worldwide figures ([Delve](https://www.delvedatabase.org/resources/how-unsafe-is-asm-counting-and-contextualizing-fatality-frequency-rates)). Almost every product performance number (94% and 98% fatigue reductions, 80% fewer near misses, 30,000 m of RQD in 15 minutes, US$430K per lost tooth) comes from the vendor selling the product. No independent validation was found for any commercial people-detection, fatigue, tooth-loss, belt-rip or core-logging system. The MSHA figure of 13 powered-haulage deaths in 2025 comes from a trade-press summary and should be checked at msha.gov. A small team that publishes an open benchmark alongside its demo would be filling a gap the incumbents have left open.

## Conclusion

The digging industries do not lack sensors. They lack cheap, vendor-neutral eyes placed at the moment of risk: when the bucket descends near a paint line, when a worker steps into an unshored trench, when a pedestrian enters a swing radius. For a Madhacks-scale team, the strongest pitch combines entries 1 and 2: a phone or cab camera that reads 811 marks, watches trench compliance, and timestamps both as evidence. It addresses the largest-cost and most-preventable-death problems together, faces no direct incumbent, and needs only a phone, colored spray paint and a sandbox to demo.

The second insight is that **data, not models, is the moat** almost everywhere on this list. No public dataset was found for locate marks, trench scenes, rig floors or USCS-labeled soil photos, and the research that exists for tunnel faces and cuttings is mostly single-site. A team that collects and labels the first such dataset, even a small one, holds something that the well-funded incumbents' closed systems do not provide. Two caveats apply to the top picks. The absence of a trench-vision product or an at-the-bucket mark checker rests on short searches, and willingness to pay was not researched for any entry. Both should be checked before committing beyond the hackathon.
